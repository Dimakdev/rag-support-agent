# Getting started

A first week that works, in the order that causes the least rework.

## Day one: the company

Settings → Company. Name, time zone, working hours, address. The time zone decides how the schedule is
drawn for everyone, and changing it later moves every job on screen, so it is worth getting right now.

## Day one: two people

Invite one dispatcher and one technician before inviting everyone. A three-person account is enough to
see the whole loop — assign, do, complete — and cheap to change your mind about.

## Day two: skills and services

Settings → Skills. Tag the things that not everyone can do. Then Settings → Services: the work you
sell, with a default duration. A job type with an honest default duration is what makes the schedule
believable later.

## Day three: import customers

Settings → Data → Import. A CSV with name, email, phone and address. Duplicates are matched on email
and flagged rather than merged, because merging two customers who are not the same is not reversible.

## Day four: one real week

Put an actual week into the schedule by hand. This is the step people skip, and it is the one that
finds the wrong assumptions — durations that are too short, travel that is unrealistic, a skill that
everybody needs.

## Day five: switch on the rest

Auto-assign, customer reminders, the accounting integration. One at a time, each after a day of
watching the previous one.

## What to leave for later

The API, territories, custom roles and SSO. None of them make the first week better, and all of them
are easier to set up once the schedule reflects reality.
